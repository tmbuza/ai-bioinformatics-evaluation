Purpose

An AI-assisted analysis may contain convincing explanations and readable code, yet still make claims that its data or methods cannot support. Evaluating it requires scientific judgment and checks that another person can follow.

Evaluating AI-Assisted Bioinformatics Analyses teaches you to inspect an analysis, verify its evidence, and communicate a justified assessment. You will practise recognizing sound reasoning, identifying errors, and explaining uncertainty through original examples drawn from biological data analysis.

This open Complex Data Insights (CDI) guide connects Omics & Bioinformatics with Artificial Intelligence. It supports research practice, independent learning, teaching, and preparation for scientific AI evaluation opportunities.

Who this guide is for

The guide is intended for researchers, postgraduate students, bioinformatics practitioners, educators, and scientific AI evaluation candidates worldwide. No particular institutional affiliation or qualification is required.

You should already be able to:

  • Read data tables and explain a biological comparison.
  • Recognize biological replication and interpret basic statistical results.
  • Read and run a short R script and locate its outputs.

You do not need expertise in every field represented here. For preparation, use the relevant CDI guide: Microbiome, RNA-seq, Single-Cell, Proteomics, or GWAS. The AI Systems guide provides supporting AI concepts.

Scientific scope

Microbiome analysis provides recurring examples grounded in CDI’s research experience, covering sample metadata, community profiles, diversity, and biological interpretation.

RNA-seq is another core area, connecting gene-expression analysis with experimental design, differential expression, and evidence-based interpretation. A guided RNA-seq case introduces these checks; a single-cell case extends the practice to cell-level workflows and annotation.

Proteomics and GWAS cases add practice with missing measurements and population structure. Together, the five cases cover major areas of omics analysis while keeping domain knowledge and method-specific checks central to each review.

What you will learn

By completing the exercises, you should be able to:

  1. Identify the scientific question and the evidence needed to answer it.
  2. Check data provenance, study design, methods, and assumptions.
  3. Verify selected outputs and compare them with reported results.
  4. Distinguish supported claims, errors, and unresolved questions.
  5. Write evidence-based feedback with justified corrections.

Every case follows the same process:

Inspect the question → check data and methods → verify results → assess claims → communicate the evaluation.

Reproducing a result does not, by itself, establish that the method or interpretation is appropriate. Your review must address both execution and scientific reasoning.

Learning sequence

Stage Focus Your output
Foundations Evaluation criteria and reproducible checks Evaluation plan and evidence log
Guided RNA-seq case Design, statistical results, and interpretation Annotated review
Microbiome case and supporting examples Community data, analytical choices, and biological claims Evidence-supported evaluation
More independent single-cell case Workflow decisions, annotation, and uncertainty Structured scientific review
Independent practice Apply the framework and prioritize findings Final evaluation report

The sequence moves from guided review toward independent judgment, with additional microbiome examples reinforcing the framework. Cases will combine correct decisions, deliberate errors, and unresolved claims; assess each against the evidence.

How to use the guide

Attempt each exercise before consulting its worked explanation. Record the claim reviewed, the check performed, the evidence found, and your proposed action. Explain the consequence of an issue instead of simply marking it wrong.

The core exercises use supplied materials; a paid AI account is not required. Simulated AI responses will be labelled. Form your own initial judgment, then document and verify any optional AI assistance you use.

R verification scripts will live in scripts/R/, with setup instructions alongside each completed workflow. Run the scripts as instructed and inspect their outputs. Rendering the book is a publishing step, not evidence that you have independently verified an analysis. Distinguish supplied outputs from results you reproduced yourself.

Guide development

The guide is being completed chapter by chapter. Case datasets, scripts, outputs, and worked explanations remain under development until their chapters identify them as available.

Your final deliverable

You will produce an evaluation report documenting your checks, findings, justified corrections, and remaining uncertainty. A reader should be able to tell what you verified and what you could not establish.

The report can support research training or a professional portfolio. Clearly label teaching data, acknowledge assistance, and describe your own contribution.

This open guide stands on its own; separate CDI practicals may provide further assignments. The materials are independent of employer assessments and institutional programmes. Completion develops demonstrable skills without guaranteeing selection, employment, or income.

Before you begin

Identify your strongest biological domain and one skill you need to strengthen. Confirm that you can run a short R script and find its output, then continue to Orientation.
